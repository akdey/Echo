# **Architectural Blueprint for an Automated Institutional-Grade Indian Equity Screener**

The modernization of the Indian capital markets represents a critical inflection point for quantitative trading architectures. As algorithmic frameworks interface with the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE), legacy screening mechanisms relying on lagging indicators are rendered obsolete. Modern market microstructure is defined by high-frequency institutional order flows, latency arbitrage, complex surveillance mechanisms, and massive domestic liquidity pools. Furthermore, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has mandated a stringent 2026 algorithmic trading framework that fundamentally alters retail and proprietary Application Programming Interface (API) participation, shifting the entire paradigm toward auditable, broker-principal routing and strict execution thresholds1.  
To deploy capital with high trust in this environment, a scanning application must transcend basic price alerts. It requires a backend logic capable of interpreting Level 3 order book dynamics to identify spoofing3, quantifying unstructured Natural Language Processing (NLP) sentiment from earnings calls to gauge momentum half-life4, evaluating insider pledging mechanics to foresee liquidity traps6, and dynamically adjusting to macroeconomic interventions by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI)8. Furthermore, the integration of deep learning foundation models, such as the Kronos K-line tokenizer9 and DreamerV3 reinforcement learning world models11, provides a zero-shot predictive capacity that fundamentally shifts the scanner from a reactive alert system to a proactive predictive engine.  
The following architectural report provides an exhaustive, master-level breakdown of the parameters, algorithms, and market mechanics required to build a compliant, highly profitable, and robust automated equity scanner for the BSE and NSE.

## **1\. Price Action and Technical Data (The "How")**

### **Candle and Bar Mechanics: Programmatic Interpretation**

Raw Open, High, Low, Close, and Volume (OHLCV) data forms the foundational matrix of price action. However, the programmatic interpretation of this data must focus on the micro-structures that reveal institutional intent, rather than simple color-coded bar directions. In the Indian equity markets, candlestick micro-structures—specifically long wicks, displacement candles, and fair value gaps (FVGs)—represent the physical footprint of liquidity sweeps. Institutional participants require substantial counter-party liquidity to execute large block orders without incurring massive slippage. To source this liquidity, the market is often manipulated to breach prominent structural levels, such as swing highs or lows, where retail stop-loss orders are densely clustered13.  
A liquidity sweep manifests as a false break of structure, engineered to trigger pending orders before an aggressive reversal. This phenomenon is distinct from a genuine breakout, which is characterized by price holding beyond the broken level and forming a new structure13. To programmatically identify a sweep, the system must analyze the relationship between the candlestick wick, the closing body, and the preceding order block. An order block is defined as the price range of the candle that forms at the start of a pivot13. When a long wick extends beyond a significant level but the body closes back within the previous range, it signals that liquidity is being harvested15. The subsequent formation of a displacement candle—a large, one-sided candle leaving behind a Fair Value Gap—confirms that institutions have absorbed the liquidity and are aggressively repricing the asset13.

### **Order Book Dynamics: Level 2 and Level 3 Surveillance**

While Level 1 data shows the Last Traded Price (LTP) and National Best Bid and Offer (NBBO), institutional scanning requires sophisticated depth analysis. Level 2 (L2) data provides the aggregated order book, displaying the total volume of resting limit orders across various price tiers3. However, L2 data is easily manipulated by high-frequency trading (HFT) algorithms that generate synthetic support and resistance walls. To achieve true trust, the system must interface with Level 3 (L3) data, which provides a granular, market-by-order (MBO) feed featuring individual order IDs, execution times, and precise cancellation timestamps3.  
Order book data reveals the true supply and demand mechanics of NSE/BSE securities. Advanced algorithms utilize L3 data to detect malicious market microstructure phenomena, specifically spoofing and iceberg orders. Spoofing involves a participant placing massive, non-bonafide limit orders to create synthetic "liquidity walls." This induces panic buying or selling among retail participants relying on L2 data, only for the spoofer to cancel the orders microseconds before execution and trade in the opposite direction20. The system logic must calculate features such as Liquidity Fade Velocity—the speed and volume of order cancellations occurring within a tight band of the current best bid or ask—to detect this predatory behavior19. Conversely, iceberg orders involve slicing a massive institutional trade into smaller, visible tranches to disguise the total volume23. When an iceberg order is active, price momentum inexplicably stalls despite high volume, as the hidden liquidity continuously absorbs aggressive market orders, rapidly refreshing at the same price level25.

### **Confluence Indicators and World Model Integrations**

To filter out the immense noise inherent in the Indian equity markets, the system must abandon standard, lagging mathematical derivatives (such as RSI, MACD, and Bollinger Bands) as primary decision drivers. Instead, next-generation systems must integrate financial time-series foundation models (TSFMs) and reinforcement learning world models to achieve true zero-shot predictive confluence.  
The Kronos foundation model represents a paradigm shift in processing OHLCV data. Unlike traditional regression models, Kronos abstracts financial K-line sequences as a discrete language9. It utilizes a technique called Binary Spherical Quantization (BSQ) to convert 6-dimensional continuous market data (OHLCVA) into discrete 20-bit binary tokens. These tokens are hierarchically structured, with the upper 10 bits forming coarse subtokens that capture the primary structure of price movements, and the lower 10 bits forming fine subtokens that capture residual precision and volatility10. Pre-trained autoregressively on a corpus of over 12 billion K-line records from 45 global exchanges, Kronos acts as a generative decoder, predicting the probabilistic trajectory of the next candlestick sequence without requiring task-specific fine-tuning9.  
Parallel to Kronos, the application architecture must deploy a DreamerV3-inspired world model for policy optimization. DreamerV3 utilizes a Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) to encode both stochastic latent states and deterministic history, constructing an internal transition matrix of the market11. Rather than generating a binary signal based on current conditions, the Latent Imagination Engine simulates hundreds of possible future outcomes extending up to 30 bars into the future12. Through Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), multiple separate agents test different execution thresholds simultaneously, heavily penalizing agents that execute during high-variance market chop. The system calculates an exact standard deviation threshold required to catch a mean-reversion reversal before it manifests on the chart12.

## **2\. Fundamental and Macro Filters (The "Why")**

### **Core Financials: Structural Viability**

A high-trust screener requires an immutable layer of fundamental analysis to prevent the automated deployment of capital into fundamentally insolvent enterprises. Technical signals, regardless of their sophistication, are meaningless if the underlying corporate structure is compromised. In the Indian equity landscape, basic metrics like Price-to-Earnings (P/E) are often skewed by one-off gains, cyclical anomalies, or aggressive accounting practices. Therefore, the system must evaluate the true operational efficiency and leverage of a company through stringent fundamental parameters.  
The algorithm must distinctly separate the fundamental requirements for long-term compounding investments versus shorter-duration swing trades. For long-term structural trades, the focus is on capital efficiency and cash generation. Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) and Return on Equity (ROE) measure how effectively management utilizes shareholder and debt capital to generate profits. Free Cash Flow (FCF) acts as the ultimate truth-teller, stripping away accounting accruals to reveal the actual cash generated by operations34. Furthermore, the system must evaluate insider alignment. Analysis of institutional data suggests a non-linear relationship between insider (promoter) ownership and firm value. Ownership levels between 5% and 15% represent a "sweet spot" indicating high alignment without the risk of management entrenchment, which often occurs when ownership exceeds 25%35.

### **Macro Sensitivities: RBI, GST, and CPI**

The screener must quantify top-down macroeconomic variables that dictate sectoral liquidity, raw material costs, and domestic consumption patterns. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Monetary Policy Committee controls the repo rate, which represents the fundamental cost of capital in the Indian economy. The repo rate dictates the trajectory of bank lending rates, home loan EMIs, and corporate borrowing costs8. Goods and Services Tax (GST) collections and restructuring—such as transitioning from a complex four-tier system to a simplified two-tier structure—directly impact consumer pricing, fiscal deficits, and inflation dynamics37. Furthermore, Consumer Price Index (CPI) data dictates the RBI's willingness to maintain an accommodative or hawkish stance.  
The market prices these macroeconomic variables directionally across varying sectors. An RBI rate hike increases the cost of borrowing, heavily depressing leveraged sectors like real estate, infrastructure, and capital goods, while simultaneously squeezing consumer discretionary spending due to higher EMIs8. Conversely, rate cuts or dovish pauses trigger rallies in these interest-rate-sensitive sectors. GST rationalizations, such as dropping essential goods into a 5% tax bracket, directly boost domestic consumption, driving up fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) and automotive sectors by effectively lowering the CPI basket costs39.

## **3\. Sentiment, News, and the "Half-Life" of Catalysts**

### **News Categorization and Sentiment Processing**

The ingestion and processing of unstructured data—specifically text from earnings transcripts, exchange filings, and financial news—contains massive predictive alpha. The modern scanner must convert narrative into quantifiable programmatic logic. Traditional sentiment analysis tools relying on generic dictionaries (such as VADER) are fundamentally inadequate for financial contexts; a phrase like "conservative approach to capital allocation" may score negatively in a generic dictionary but is heavily bullish in a corporate finance context4.  
To effectively process this data, the system must deploy domain-tuned NLP models, such as FinBERT or financial Longformer architectures, capable of handling extended token lengths (up to 4096 tokens) required for full earnings call transcripts5. A critical programmatic task is the segmentation of earnings calls into *Prepared Remarks* and the *Q\&A Session*. Prepared remarks are highly scripted, legally sanitized, and inherently optimistic. The true alpha lies in the Q\&A session, where unscripted responses to analyst probing reveal actual management sentiment, hedging terminology, and forward guidance vulnerabilities4.  
The "half-life" of a catalyst refers to how long the momentum generated by a news event will sustain itself before mean-reverting. Structural shifts, such as sustained earnings surprises backed by positive forward guidance, have long half-lives that can carry a stock for an entire fiscal quarter. Conversely, isolated global geopolitical shocks often induce sharp, algorithmic panic selling with extremely short half-lives, presenting rapid mean-reversion buying opportunities once the initial emotional liquidation subsides.

## **4\. Smart Money and Insider Trends (The "Under the Radar" Data)**

### **FII and DII Daily Flow Dynamics**

Institutional capital flows are the primary driver of market liquidity and index-level momentum in India. Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs)—which include global hedge funds, pension funds, and sovereign wealth funds—manage trillions of dollars and dictate short-term to medium-term momentum. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs)—primarily mutual funds fueled by retail Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs), banks, and insurance companies—act as structural buffers, providing underlying baseline liquidity42.  
Sustained FII buying injects massive liquidity, pushing large-cap stocks and indices (Nifty 50, Sensex) into aggressive uptrends. Their capital allocation heavily favors index-heavy sectors such as Banking, IT, and Financial Services. FII selling, driven by global risk aversion or rising US interest rates, causes sharp corrections and currency depreciation42. However, the Indian market has evolved structurally; strong DII buying now acts as a counterbalance. When DIIs aggressively buy during FII sell-offs, they absorb the foreign supply, cushioning the market crash and altering the traditional correlation between FII outflows and total market collapse44.

### **Exchange Disclosures: Bulk Deals, Block Deals, and Promoter Pledging**

Tracking the physical transfer of massive share quantities and the financial maneuvers of insiders provides direct insight into "smart money" positioning. The NSE and BSE differentiate between two types of massive trades. A *Bulk Deal* occurs when a single transaction exceeds 0.5% of a company's total listed equity, executed during normal trading hours46. A *Block Deal* involves a minimum of 5 lakh shares or ₹10 crore in value and is executed in a specialized, pre-arranged trading window (typically at a price limited to \+/- 1% of the previous close) to minimize market impact48. Bulk deals often cause immediate price volatility as retail participants react to the live order book disruption, while block deals represent silent, pre-negotiated transfers between large entities47.  
More critical than deal flow is the analysis of Promoter Pledging. Promoter pledging occurs when company founders use their equity holdings as collateral to secure loans from banks or Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs)6. SEBI mandates strict quarterly disclosures regarding these pledges. The market heavily discounts companies with high pledge ratios because it inherently ties the company's financial stability to short-term stock price fluctuations. Lenders apply a "haircut" to the collateral value; if the stock price falls below the lender's threshold, it triggers a margin call. If promoters cannot provide additional capital, the lenders aggressively dump the pledged shares on the open market, causing a catastrophic, self-fulfilling stock crash and potentially leading to a hostile takeover or loss of management control7.

### **Surveillance Frameworks: ASM and GSM Integration**

To protect capital, the application must natively integrate SEBI's regulatory watchlists. SEBI and the exchanges enforce the Additional Surveillance Measure (ASM) and Graded Surveillance Measure (GSM) frameworks to curb speculative bubbles, limit excessive volatility, and protect retail investors51.  
Inclusion in these lists is deterministic, based on objective criteria such as abnormal high-low price variation over short periods, sudden volume spikes disconnected from historical averages, high client concentration among the top 25 traders, or extreme P/E deviations combined with low market capitalization51. Inclusion acts as a massive, intentional liquidity drain. As stocks progress through the stages of ASM (Short-Term or Long-Term) and GSM, exchanges impose brutal restrictions: 100% upfront margin requirements, slashing of daily price bands down to 5% or 2%, the complete disabling of intraday leverage, and ultimately shifting the stock to Trade-to-Trade (T2T) settlement, which requires 100% delivery of shares52. For GSM Stage III and IV, trading may even be restricted to once a week with massive Additional Surveillance Deposits (ASD) required from buyers53.

## **5\. Risk, Trust, and Application Architecture**

### **Algorithmic Sector Rotation and Trap Detection**

To prevent capital erosion from false breakouts and sector decay, the screener must employ deep-learning-based sector rotation logic. The Indian market moves in predictable, six-phase cyclical rotations: Bullish, Warning, Distribution, Bearish, Recovery, and Accumulation57. While retail traders chase lagging breakouts, an automated system must utilize mathematical modeling to identify when capital is shifting from cyclical to defensive sectors.  
Utilizing a dual-layer Markov Decision Process (MDP) structure combined with Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and graph convolution neural networks, the system can map the interconnectedness of NSE sectors58. The multi-agent trend prediction layer analyzes time-series data to learn the magnitude of sector changes, while the multi-source fusion layer integrates the NLP sentiment data to anticipate macroeconomic rotations before they manifest in price action58. By analyzing breadth indicators and multi-timeframe Trend Strength Indicators (TSI), the algorithm distinguishes between broad-based institutional moves and anomalous spikes driven by a few large-cap constituents57.

### **Execution and Compliance: The SEBI 2026 Algorithmic Framework**

The ultimate pillar of trust for an algorithmic application is absolute regulatory compliance. The entire Indian broker-API landscape underwent a tectonic regulatory shift, with strict enforcement beginning April 1, 2026, aimed at preventing market manipulation and ensuring traceability1. SEBI formally abolished "open APIs," fundamentally altering how retail and proprietary systems interface with exchanges. Under the new framework, brokers are legally designated as Principals, assuming full responsibility for all API traffic, while the algorithmic software acts as the Agent60. The framework classifies algorithms into "White Box" (transparent, rule-based logic) and "Black Box" (proprietary logic requiring the provider to hold a SEBI Research Analyst license)2.  
To ensure the backend application remains legally deployable, resilient, and immune to broker-level rejection or account suspension, the system architect must hard-code several strict parameters into the execution layer.

By integrating predictive transformer models (Kronos)9, decoding Level 3 order flow to avoid spoofing traps3, mapping the trajectory of institutional capital42, and adhering flawlessly to the SEBI 2026 execution mandates2, the system achieves true institutional-grade reliability.
